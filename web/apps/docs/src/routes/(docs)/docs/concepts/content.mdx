# Concepts

Plexor groups everything you run inside a single hierarchy: three
levels, each with its own job. The split is deliberate — it is the
simplest structure that lets a team of five to fifteen share a
deployment with other teams without bolting on a custom access layer.

## Organisation

The **organisation** is the top of the hierarchy. Every Plexor
deployment has exactly one. Users, teams and resources all live inside
it. The organisation is what your identity provider talks to, what an
invoice covers, and what the audit log scopes to.

Multi-organisation deploys are out of scope in the current release —
they land in a later phase.

## Team

A **team** is a group of people who share access to a slice of the
deployment — typically five to fifteen users. Roles (admin, operator,
viewer) can be scoped to a team, so a "platform team" can hold the
keys to destructive actions while every other team runs their own
workloads without that reach.

Team-scoped endpoints land in the next phase; teams are first-class
entities now.

## Folder

A **folder** is where resources actually live: virtual machines,
Kubernetes clusters, app installs. Most resources in Plexor are
folder-scoped by default. Folders let one team separate concerns —
one folder per environment (`dev`, `staging`, `prod`) or per project
(`project-alpha`, `project-beta`) without spinning up a new
organisation each time.

## The resource scope hierarchy

A resource sits at one of three levels. Which level it lives at
decides who can see it.

| Resource lives at... | Who can see it |
|---|---|
| Organisation | Everyone in the organisation. Shared infrastructure — a shared `k8s-prod` cluster, a shared CI database. Writes are admin-only and audit-logged. |
| Organisation + Team | Everyone in the team. Team-shared services and databases. |
| Organisation + Team + Folder | Everyone in the folder. The default for most workloads. |

The levels cascade downward: a folder-scoped resource is implicitly
team- and organisation-scoped too, and a team-scoped resource is
implicitly organisation-scoped.

Resources default to **folder-scoped**. Organisation- and team-scoped
resources are opt-in for things that genuinely need to be shared —
the creation path for those is admin-only and writes leave an
audit-trail entry.

## Why three levels and not one big namespace

Without the hierarchy, multi-team deployments either land everything
in one shared namespace (everyone sees everyone else's resources,
role bindings balloon) or layer a custom access system on top of a
flat namespace. The three-level hierarchy is the smallest structure
that lets an operator express "I want this visible to the team but
not to the rest of the organisation" without writing any
authorisation code themselves.
