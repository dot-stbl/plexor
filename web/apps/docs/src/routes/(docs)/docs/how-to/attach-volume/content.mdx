---
title: "Attach a volume"
description: "Provision a block volume, attach it to a workload, and expand it without downtime."

A volume is a block device you provision from Plexor's storage
backend and attach to a workload. The workload's guest OS sees the
volume as a raw disk; formatting and mounting are the guest's
responsibility.

This page walks through creating a volume, attaching it to a
running workload, and growing the volume without downtime.

## Step 1 — Create the volume

Open **Storage → Volumes** and click **Create volume**.

<ScreenshotPlaceholder caption="Volume create dialog with the size picker" />

The dialog asks for two things:

- **Name** — kebab-case, unique per cluster.
- **Size** — gibibytes. The minimum is 1 GiB; the maximum depends on
  the storage backend (16 TiB on Ceph RBD, 1 TiB on local-lvm).

Click **Create**. The volume starts in `Available` state. There is no
quota block at create time — quotas check at workload-attach, not at
volume-create — because a detached volume uses only disk space, not
the workloads-per-folder cap.

## Step 2 — Attach the volume

Open the volume's detail view and click **Attach**. Pick the
workload you want to attach to.

<Callout type="warning">

A volume can be attached to **one workload at a time**. Plexor
refuses to attach a volume that is already attached elsewhere.
Detach the volume from the first workload before re-attaching to a
second; the **Force detach** option exists for break-glass but
should not be the default — it leaves the second workload with a
device that may have stale filesystem metadata.

</Callout>

The attach is recorded as a workload-side event; the volume's
state moves to `In use`. The workload sees a new raw device —
typically `/dev/vdb` on VMs and a configured mount source on
container workloads.

## Step 3 — Format and mount (in the workload)

Inside the workload, the new device is unformatted raw storage.
The exact steps depend on the workload's guest OS; on a recent
Linux:

```bash
# Find the new device
lsblk

# Create a filesystem (ext4 in this example)
sudo mkfs.ext4 /dev/vdb

# Mount it
sudo mkdir -p /mnt/data
sudo mount /dev/vdb /mnt/data

# Make the mount persistent across reboots
echo '/dev/vdb /mnt/data ext4 defaults,nofail 0 2' | sudo tee -a /etc/fstab
```

The `nofail` option is important — without it, a missing volume at
boot will hang the workload's boot. Plexor does not guarantee that
an attached volume is present at boot time; the workload should be
resilient to that.

## Step 4 — Resize the volume (no downtime)

If the workload runs out of disk, you can resize the volume without
stopping it. Open the volume's detail view and click **Resize**.
Enter the new size in GiB; the new size must be greater than the
current size (shrinking is not supported).

Resize expands the volume's backing storage inside Plexor. The
guest OS does **not** automatically grow the filesystem. After the
resize completes, in the workload:

```bash
# Tell the kernel the device is now bigger
echo 1 | sudo tee /sys/block/vdb/device/rescan

# Grow the filesystem to fill the device
sudo resize2fs /dev/vdb
```

Filesystem growth is online — the workload stays up, the mount is
preserved, and the new space is immediately available.

<Callout type="info">

The grow-the-filesystem step is on the guest, not on Plexor.
Plexor only knows how to grow the backing volume; the filesystem
inside is the guest's concern. Skipping the guest step is the
most common resize mistake — the workload reports the volume as
the new size but the filesystem as the old size, which causes
"no space left on device" errors.

</Callout>

## Detach the volume

When the workload no longer needs the volume, unmount it inside
the workload first (`sudo umount /mnt/data`), then open the
volume's detail view and click **Detach**. Plexor refuses to
detach a volume that is still mounted; the guest must release the
mount first.

## What to do next

- **Take a snapshot** of the volume before destructive operations.
  Snapshot support is a separate concept (not yet in the current
  release; tracked in the console as a "Soon" entry).
- **Set a quota** for volumes in the folder if the team has a habit
  of out-growing their allocation — see **Set up quotas**.
