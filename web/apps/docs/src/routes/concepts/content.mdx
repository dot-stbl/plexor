# Concepts

Plexor splits resources across three levels. The split is deliberate —
each level answers a different question, and the resource scope hierarchy
that falls out of it is what makes multi-team, multi-environment
deployments tractable without bolting on a custom access layer.

## Organisation

The **organisation** is the top-level billing and authentication
boundary. Every Plexor deployment has exactly one organisation; users,
teams, and resources all live inside it. The organisation is what an
identity provider talks to, what an invoice covers, and what an
audit log scopes to.

Multi-org deploys are isolated — cross-organisation sharing is a
phase-2 feature and is intentionally absent in v1.

## Team

A **team** is a permission aggregation. Most teams in a Plexor
deployment are 5–15 people who own a slice of the platform together.
Role bindings (admin, operator, viewer) can be scoped to a team — a
"team-zero admins" role binding lets the cluster platform team lock
out everyone else from destructive actions while still letting other
teams run their own workloads.

Teams are first-class entities in v1; team-scoped endpoints land in
phase 2.

## Folder

A **folder** is the resource namespace. Where the actual workloads
(VirtualMachines, Kubernetes clusters, app instances) live by default.
Folders give operators a way to separate concerns inside a team —
one folder per environment (`dev`, `staging`, `prod`) or per project
(`project-alpha`, `project-beta`) without spinning up a new
organisation each time.

## The resource scope hierarchy

A resource in Plexor sits at one of three levels, expressed by which
of `OrgId`, `TeamId`, and `FolderId` are set on it:

| Scope | Visible to |
|-------|-----------|
| `OrgId` only (`TeamId` + `FolderId` null) | Everyone in the organisation. Used for shared infrastructure — a `k8s-prod` cluster, a shared CI database. Admin-only writes, audit-logged. |
| `OrgId` + `TeamId` (`FolderId` null) | Everyone in the team. Used for team-shared services and databases. |
| `OrgId` + `TeamId` + `FolderId` | Everyone in the folder. The default for per-project resources — most workloads land here. |

The empty tiers cascade downward: a folder-scoped resource is
implicitly team- and org-scoped too, and a team-scoped resource is
implicitly org-scoped. The hierarchy is **3 levels of nullable
fields**, not a foreign-key chain — the cardinality of the
combination is what selects the scope.

### Defaults

Resources default to **folder-scoped**. Org-level and team-level
scopes are opt-in for the resources that genuinely need to be
shared, and the creation path for those scopes is admin-only with an
audit log line.

## What the hierarchy buys you

Without the hierarchy, multi-team deployments either land everything
in one big namespace (everyone sees everyone else's resources, role
bindings balloon) or get a custom access layer bolted on top of a
flat namespace. The hierarchy is the simplest structure that lets
operators express "I want this visible to the team but not to the
rest of the org" without writing a single line of authorisation
logic.

The cost is one nullable field on every resource table. That's the
trade.
